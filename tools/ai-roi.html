Value AI by time returned, not by hype

For most small businesses, the first real return from AI is time: drafting emails and proposals, summarising documents, answering routine customer questions, cleaning spreadsheets and producing first drafts of marketing content. The question is simple — how many hours a week does it give back, and what is an hour worth?

Use a loaded hourly cost: wages plus payroll taxes and benefits, or for owners, the rate you would bill a client. Be conservative about hours saved; measure for two weeks before and after adoption if you can.

Where small teams see results first

  • Customer support triage and FAQ answers, with a human approving edge cases.
  • Bookkeeping categorisation and invoice follow-ups.
  • Content repurposing: one long article into posts, emails and scripts.
  • Sales prep: research summaries before calls.
Time saved only becomes money if it is redeployed — into selling, serving more customers, or not hiring before you have to.

FAQ

Is free AI good enough?

Free tiers are fine for trials. Paid plans usually add privacy controls, higher limits and team features that matter for business data.

What about risks?

Keep a human reviewing anything customer-facing, avoid pasting sensitive personal data into tools without a clear data policy, and check facts.
