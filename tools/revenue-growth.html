Why CAGR beats "we grew 117%"

Total growth hides time. Growing 117% in three years is very different from 117% in one. The compound annual growth rate (CAGR) smooths the journey into one yearly rate, so you can compare periods, products and competitors fairly.

CAGR = (ending value ÷ starting value)^(1 ÷ years) − 1. Doubling time uses the same rate: divide the natural log of 2 by the natural log of (1 + CAGR). The "rule of 72" — 72 divided by the growth percentage — is a handy mental shortcut.

Use projections carefully

Projecting the same CAGR forward is a scenario, not a forecast. Growth usually slows as a business gets bigger. Run a conservative and an aggressive case and plan cash for the conservative one.

FAQ

Can CAGR be negative?

Yes — if revenue fell over the period, CAGR shows the average yearly rate of decline.

Is CAGR good for monthly data?

Use months as the period and you get a compound monthly growth rate instead.
