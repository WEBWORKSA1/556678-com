The ratio that decides whether growth is healthy

Lifetime value (LTV) is the gross profit a customer generates before they leave. Customer acquisition cost (CAC) is everything you spend in sales and marketing divided by the customers it produced. A ratio of about 3:1 is a widely used benchmark for subscription businesses: enough margin to fund growth without starving the company.

Equally important is CAC payback — how many months of gross profit it takes to earn back the acquisition cost. Short payback means growth funds itself; long payback means growth consumes cash.

Improving the ratio

  • Reduce churn with better onboarding — retention compounds LTV faster than price increases.
  • Raise ARPU with annual plans, add-ons and tiered pricing.
  • Lower CAC with referrals, partnerships, SEO and content that keeps working after you publish it.

FAQ

Does this work for non-subscription businesses?

Yes. Use average monthly gross profit per active customer and the share of customers who stop buying each month.

Why use gross margin and not revenue?

Revenue overstates value; the cost of serving the customer has to come out first.
