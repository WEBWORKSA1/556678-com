Most launch budgets forget the reserve

Founders are good at listing what they need to open and bad at budgeting for the months after opening, when costs are running but customers are still discovering you. That gap is where many first-year failures happen. U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics data has long shown that roughly one in five new businesses does not reach its second year.

This calculator combines one-time costs with a reserve measured in months of operating expenses. Six months is a sensible minimum for most service businesses; product, retail and hospitality concepts often need more.

Where to find the money

Personal savings remain the most common source of startup capital, followed by friends-and-family money, credit, microloans and grants. See our funding options guide for the trade-offs of each.

FAQ

Should I count my own salary?

If you need to draw a salary to live, include it in monthly operating costs — otherwise your reserve is too small.

What are commonly forgotten costs?

Insurance deposits, payment-terminal hardware, professional fees, sales tax registration and the cost of your own time.
