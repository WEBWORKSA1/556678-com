Margin and markup are not the same number

Markup is profit measured against cost. Margin is profit measured against price. An item that costs $50 and sells for $100 has a 100% markup but a 50% margin. Confusing the two is one of the most common pricing mistakes: owners who "add 50%" to cost end up with a 33% margin and wonder where the money went.

What is a healthy margin?

It depends on the model. Software and digital products often run gross margins above 70%. Service businesses commonly land between 40% and 60% once labour is counted as cost. Retail and food typically sit lower, which is why volume and inventory turns matter so much in those sectors. Compare yourself to your own business model, not to someone else's.

Rule of thumb: if your gross margin cannot cover your fixed costs at realistic volume, no amount of marketing will fix it. Fix price or cost first.

FAQ

How do I turn a target margin into a price?

Price = cost ÷ (1 − target margin). For a 60% margin on a $20 cost: 20 ÷ 0.4 = $50.

Should shipping be included in cost?

Yes, if you pay it. Anything you spend only because a sale happened belongs in cost of goods.
