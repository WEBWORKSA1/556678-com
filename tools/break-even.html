How the break-even point works

Every sale you make does two jobs. First it pays back the variable cost of that sale — the materials, packaging, shipping or payment fee that only exists because you sold something. Whatever is left over is the contribution margin, and that is the money that chips away at your fixed costs: rent, salaries, software, insurance. The break-even point is the moment the pile of contribution margins equals the pile of fixed costs.

The formula is simple: break-even units = fixed costs ÷ (price − variable cost per unit). If your fixed costs are $6,000 a month, you sell at $80 and each unit costs $32 to deliver, each sale contributes $48 and you need 125 sales to cover the month.

Three levers that move it

  • Raise price. A 10% price increase often cuts the break-even point by far more than 10%, because the whole increase lands in contribution margin.
  • Cut variable cost. Negotiating suppliers, bundling shipping or switching payment processors changes every single future sale.
  • Trim fixed cost. The slowest lever, but it lowers the bar permanently.

Use the profit-target field to see how many sales you need to pay yourself — break-even is the floor, not the goal.

FAQ

Is break-even calculated monthly or yearly?

Either works as long as fixed costs and the sales period match. Monthly is the most useful for planning because it lines up with rent and payroll.

What if I sell many products?

Use a weighted average price and variable cost across your product mix, or run the calculator once per product line.
