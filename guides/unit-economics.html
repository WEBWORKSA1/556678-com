Unit Economics for Non-Finance Founders: LTV, CAC and Payback

By the 556678 Editorial Team · 6 min read · Updated November 2026

Understand the three numbers that tell you whether growth makes you money or burns it — with worked examples.

Unit economics answer one question: do we make money on each customer? If the answer is yes, growth is a matter of finding more customers. If the answer is no, growth just makes losses bigger. Three numbers tell you which situation you're in.

Customer acquisition cost (CAC)

Add up everything spent to win customers in a period — ads, sales salaries and commissions, marketing tools, agency fees, event costs — and divide by the number of new customers in that period. Be honest: leaving out salaries is the most common way CAC gets understated.

Lifetime value (LTV)

For a subscription business: average monthly revenue per customer × gross margin ÷ monthly churn rate. A customer paying $49 a month at 75% gross margin, with 4% monthly churn, generates about $919 of gross profit over their lifetime. For repeat-purchase businesses, use average order gross profit × expected number of orders.

CAC payback

CAC ÷ monthly gross profit per customer. If you spend $220 to acquire that $49 customer, payback is about six months. Shorter payback means growth can fund itself; longer payback means growth needs outside cash.

Benchmarks and judgement

An LTV-to-CAC ratio around 3:1 is a widely used benchmark for healthy subscription businesses, with payback under 12 months. These are rules of thumb, not laws: a business with plenty of cash may accept longer payback to grow faster; a bootstrapped one should aim much shorter.

Segment, don't average

Averages hide the truth. Calculate unit economics by channel (referral customers are usually far cheaper than paid-ad customers) and by plan or product. You'll often find one segment carrying the business and another quietly losing money.

Improving the numbers

  • Retention first: cutting churn from 4% to 3% raises LTV by a third.
  • Expansion revenue: upgrades and add-ons raise ARPU without new acquisition cost.
  • Cheaper channels: referrals, partnerships and content lower blended CAC over time.
