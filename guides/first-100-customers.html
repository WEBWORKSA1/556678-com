How to Win Your First 100 Customers

By the 556678 Editorial Team · 7 min read · Updated November 2026

A step-by-step plan for early traction: direct outreach, communities, partnerships, referrals and content that compounds.

Your first hundred customers rarely come from a big launch. They come from a series of small, deliberate, often manual actions. The good news: you don't need a large budget, just a clear target customer and a repeatable weekly rhythm.

Step 1: Narrow the target

"Small businesses" is not a target. "Independent physiotherapy clinics in Ontario with two to five practitioners" is. A narrow definition tells you where to find people, what language they use and what problem keeps them up at night. You can widen it later.

Step 2: Talk to 20 people before you sell to them

Book short conversations — not pitches — with people in your target group. Ask how they currently solve the problem, what it costs them and what they've tried. Write down their exact words; they become your website copy and ad headlines.

Step 3: Do things that don't scale

  • Personal outreach: 10 thoughtful, individual messages a day beats 1,000 templated emails.
  • Show up where they already are: trade associations, local business groups, niche online communities. Contribute first; sell later.
  • Onboard by hand: set up the first customers yourself. You'll learn what confuses people and earn loyal advocates.

Step 4: Borrow audiences through partnerships

Find businesses that serve the same customer but don't compete with you. An accountant and a payroll provider, a wedding photographer and a florist, a web designer and a copywriter. Offer co-hosted workshops, bundled offers or simple referral swaps.

Step 5: Turn customers into a referral engine

Ask for referrals at the moment of delight — right after a win, not in a quarterly email. Make it easy with a short message they can forward, and reward both sides where it's appropriate for your industry.

Step 6: Publish content that answers real questions

Every question from your Step 2 conversations is a potential article, video or tool. Content compounds: a useful guide published today can bring customers for years through search. Free calculators and checklists are especially effective because people bookmark and share them.

A weekly traction rhythm

  1. Monday: 50 personal outreach messages scheduled for the week.
  2. Tuesday: one community contribution or event.
  3. Wednesday: one partnership conversation.
  4. Thursday: publish one helpful piece of content.
  5. Friday: review numbers — conversations, trials, customers — and adjust.
Track one leading metric (conversations booked) and one lagging metric (paying customers). If conversations rise and customers don't, fix the offer; if neither rises, fix the outreach.
